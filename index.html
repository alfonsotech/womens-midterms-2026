The backdrop: Democrats won control of both chambers. As of Wednesday afternoon they held 54 Senate seats to 44, a gain of seven, with Alaska and Mississippi still uncalled, and at least 237 House seats with 59 races left to call. Party control is context here, not the story. The story is who won, what voters decided about women's bodies and trans lives, and how women cast their ballots. (The Guardian, Reuters, AP)

A Record Year for Women Governors

Ohio, Georgia, Tennessee, and Minnesota will have women governors for the first time. Georgia's is the first Black woman to lead any state, and New Mexico's is the first Native American woman.

101women will hold statewide executive office, a record (CAWP)

Trans Women Won in States That Had Never Elected One

Alabama, Michigan, Washington, Wisconsin, and Florida elected their first trans women, and Georgia elected its first nonbinary lawmaker. Rep. Sarah McBride, the first trans woman in Congress, won a second term.

They Ran Where No Democrat Had in Years

Bella Bautista, the first trans woman to win a legislative primary in Georgia, lost in House District 14, a seat no Democrat had contested since 2010. In Kansas, Bobby Robertson Jr., a trans woman and financial analyst, lost House District 73 after becoming the first Democrat to run for it in 10 years.

Abortion Rights Mostly Held. Trans Rights Mostly Didn't.

Voters protected abortion rights in Nevada, Virginia, and Missouri, but kept Idaho's near-total ban. Most measures restricting trans people passed or were leading; the one that failed was bundled with an abortion ban.

3 of 4abortion measures went the way abortion-rights supporters wanted

Idaho is the setback: voters kept one of the country's strictest bans, so it will not join the red states that used the ballot to restore abortion access. Missouri is the mirror image. Two years after voters there struck down the state's ban, they refused the legislature's attempt to bring it back, and in doing so also rejected new limits on gender-affirming care.

Sources: NBC News ballot tracker, Yahoo News, Idaho Capital Sun, GLAAD fact sheet. Shares are as of Wednesday afternoon.

How Women Voted

Women were 53% of the electorate and backed Democratic House candidates by 19 points. Men split almost evenly.
